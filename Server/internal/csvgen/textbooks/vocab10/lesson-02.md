## Texts
### Text 1 — The Family
Family plays a crucial role in shaping who we are. Our immediate family consists of parents and siblings, while our extended family includes grandparents, aunts, uncles, and cousins. Family relationships can be complex, and sibling rivalry is common, especially during childhood. Sometimes, challenges like divorce can change family structures, resulting in single-parent families or blended families, where parents remarry and create stepfamilies. These changes affect family dynamics, as children adjust to new roles and relationships. In some cases, family members become estranged, losing touch due to conflicts or misunderstandings. However, a close-knit family can provide emotional support during difficult times.

A person's upbringing greatly influences their values and behavior, and parental guidance helps children navigate life's challenges. However, an overprotective parent might make it difficult for a child to become independent. The generation gap between parents and children can lead to differences in opinions and values, sometimes causing disagreements. Marriage also introduces new relationships with in-laws, which can be either supportive or challenging. Despite differences, families provide a sense of belonging and security. Understanding and communication are key to maintaining strong family bonds, regardless of the family structure.
